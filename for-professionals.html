Questions

What practitioners ask first.

Is Atlas a therapeutic or clinical tool?

No. It handles logistics and planning — finances, scheduling, admin, guides. It doesn't provide counselling, legal advice, or any form of professional support. It's a practical organiser, not a clinical intervention.

What happens to client data?

Everything runs in the browser. No account, no backend, no data leaving the device. Nothing is stored anywhere except the user's own browser. You have no access to it, and neither does anyone else.

Can I recommend this to clients in contested proceedings?

Atlas has no accounts and no server copy of anyone's data, so there is nothing for us to hand over and nothing the other party can log into. What a client enters lives in their browser, on their device. That device, and anything they print or export from it, is theirs to manage and may still be requested through normal legal processes.

Who built this?

Johnnie Crawford — a separated parent and brand strategist based in NSW. Built it because nothing practical existed for Australians going through this. Not a lawyer, not a therapist. A parent who needed it.

Is it Australian-specific?

Yes. Child support formula, Centrelink, ATO, Services Australia, family law 2025 — all content is AU-specific. It won't recommend US or UK resources, and the financial calculations use the Australian CSA formula.

What does it cost my clients?

$79 once. No subscription, no account, no ongoing commitment. They pay once, access everything indefinitely. There's nothing to cancel and no tier to upgrade.
